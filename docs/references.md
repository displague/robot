# References (external projects to borrow test scenarios and tooling from)

Added at P-008. One line each on what the project is and what we would take from it. Summaries
are from the project pages on 2026-10-25; check the repositories before relying on details.

| Project | What it is | What we take from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [AlessioPagliai/primo](https://github.com/AlessioPagliai/primo) | Open-source 1.40 m humanoid, still in design: 3D-printed structure, 30 RobStride quasi-direct-drive actuators, Jetson AGX Thor, ~45 kg estimated. CC0 except the Isaac Lab task code (BSD-3). Walking policy trained in Isaac Lab reaches ~87 % survival on rough terrain with stairs, no pushes or domain randomization yet. | The Isaac Lab locomotion task layout (observations, rewards, terrain curriculum) as a template for our Phase 6 training scenarios; a realistic mass/actuator comparison point for a printed humanoid at the other end of the cost scale. |
| [Rhoban/microban](https://github.com/Rhoban/microban) | ~30 cm 3D-printable humanoid from Rhoban (LaBRI, Bordeaux), ~$550-600, 19 Dynamixel XL330 servos, Raspberry Pi Zero 2W with Pollen robot hat, two 18650 cells. Software GPL v3, hardware CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Has taken first steps; keyboard/gamepad control. | A cheap, printed, walking biped from a RoboCup team: their foot design, walk engine conventions and controller structure are the nearest proven relatives of our Phase 6a static walk, at one fifth the scale. |
| [robodienst.com](https://robodienst.com/) | Presents itself as free tools for humanoid robots. The page content was not readable by the fetcher used here; revisit in a browser. | To be determined after a manual look. |
| [NVIDIA/DreamDojo](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DreamDojo) | NVIDIA's open-source interactive world model trained on 44k hours of egocentric human video, post-trained for robots; 2B and 14B checkpoints, GR-1 teleoperation datasets, ~10 FPS long-horizon generation after distillation. Apache-2.0. | Much later: a video-based world model for manipulation policy evaluation once the upper body does teleoperated tasks (Phase 3-4). Not a locomotion tool. |
| [huggingface/lerobot](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot) (added P-010) | Hugging Face's open-source robot-learning library: datasets in a common format (`LeRobotDataset`), imitation and RL policies (ACT, Diffusion Policy, VLA-style models such as pi0 and SmolVLA), teleoperation and recording tools, and a hardware layer where a robot is a bus of named motors plus cameras; the SO-100/SO-101 printed arms are its reference hardware. Apache-2.0. Summary from memory of the project; confirm against the repository. | The generic "DOF list plus camera inputs" policy interface the prompt asked about: a LeRobot robot class wrapping our ESP32 CAN nodes (joint angles from the AS5600s, line tension and slip flags from `13` as extra observation channels) would let the recorded teleoperation data and the standard policies run unchanged. Manipulation first (Phase 3-4); locomotion policies come from Isaac Lab. |
| `../ai-embedded-dynamic-diversity` (sibling repository, added P-010) | The user's PyTorch research scaffold for a portable "model core" that adapts to anonymous long-lived I/O channels across embodiments (hexapod 10 DOF, car, drone, `polymorph120`, `humanoid120`: 120 controls and 12 sensor channels, with a human-form compliance profile). Trained in an abstract 3D+time world; RTX 5080 training, Pi 5 deployment; `uv` tooling; smoke-test CI. | What the prompt suggests: this robot is a concrete, physically grounded embodiment for that core. The mapping is direct: `cad/skeleton.json` gives the DOF list and limits (32 body DOF plus 14 hand and 4 face in the table, against `humanoid120`'s 120 controls), and `sim/robot.xml` gives a harness where "actuator" means a winch with a current limit, a clutch and two lines that can go slack, so a policy learns the real effect of a control channel rather than an abstract one. The `strain` and `pressure` sensor channels of `humanoid120` have natural sources here (line tension, sole force). Done at P-011: branch `feat/robot-tendon-harness` there (worktree `../ai-embedded-dynamic-diversity-robot`) adds `sim/robot_tendon.py`, which loads this repo's `tools/sim_tendon.py` and registers the embodiment `humanoid_tendon32` (32 controls; sensor families joint_angle, joint_rate, gear_angle, strain, slack, clutch_slip, lock, current, imu, pressure, com), plus `add-sim robot-tendon` and tests. The coupling stays one-way (that repo imports this one).; P-021: `humanoid_tendon32` merged to master (PR #33); PPO with the champion core as the actor, behaviour cloning from this robot's controller, Roboparty RPO as a second body, merged as PR #36 and released as v0.3.0 (issues #48-#51 for the next steps) |
| [Roboparty roboto_origin](https://github.com/Roboparty/roboto_origin) (added P-016) | A DIY humanoid of quasi-direct-drive CAN motors on CNC aluminium (about 34 kg, 23 actuated joints, BOM about 49,700 CNY) that walks and runs. Mixed licences: deployment, firmware, navigation, teleoperation GPL-3.0; the training module roboparty_train BSD-3-Clause; the robot description CERN-OHL-W-2.0. | Its training recipe (delayed PD actuators, reward terms, domain randomisation, observation history, MuJoCo sim-to-sim checks) informs the sibling's stand-in training; its robot description is the second body there (`rl/rpo_body.py`, pinned commit, licence and notice in `third_party/roboparty_rpo`). Nothing GPL is used. |
| [Agent Hub](https://ah.meyu.us/) (added P-012) | The user's multi-agent coordination hub: workspaces hold records (findings, proposals, artifacts), tasks and notes; agents join with declared capabilities and claim tasks. This session joined as `Claude Code (robot)`. The owner created the `robot` workspace on 2026-10-27 (agents only get bounded ephemeral workspaces) and added this agent as a contributor; `set_charter` needs hub:admin, so the proposed purpose sits in `records/robot-workspace-purpose-proposed-20261027` there for the owner to apply. | In the robot workspace: tasks `onefoot-hold-hip-roll-60` (done at P-013, `15`), `first-static-step` (done at P-014, `16`), `isaac-lab-conversion`, `policy-training-humanoid-tendon32`, `w-mini-25mm-cad-presets`, each with the capabilities a worker needs. Seed records written in the home workspace, tagged `project:robot`, moved into the robot workspace by the owner (move request `mr-fcce8291afc60015`): `records/robot-project-overview-20261026` (what the project is, where things live, state at P-011), `records/robot-balance-gauntlet-p011-20261026` (the results of `14`), `records/robot-next-steps-20261026` (candidate tasks: one-foot hold, static step, Isaac Lab, policy training, W-mini presets). Once the workspace exists, each next step becomes a hub task other agents can claim. |
| [AgiBot World](https://agibot-world.com/) / [AgiBotWorld2026](https://huggingface.co/datasets/agibot-world/AgiBotWorld2026) (added P-043) | AgiBot's real-world robot dataset: 17.5 TB, about 737k rows of long-horizon dual-arm tasks (shop restocking, home) on the wheeled G2, collected free-form; multi-camera video, joint and effector states and actions, subtask and skill labels, 2D boxes; LeRobot v2.1 layout, a 7 GB sample; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Related: GO-1 (their vision-language-action model, open checkpoints), Genie Sim 3.0 (Isaac Sim, with G2 URDF/USD and digital twins of the scenes). From the pages on 2026-10-30. | Not near-term. It has no legs, balance, locomotion, tendons, forces or tactile data, so nothing for the stand, step, walk or wind work. Later: its annotation scheme (subtask spans, skill labels) and LeRobot layout as the template for our own teleoperation recordings (phase 3-4); arm-task scenarios and the G2 URDF as a reach and workspace comparison; Genie Sim alongside `isaac-lab-conversion`. Its share-alike, non-commercial licence carries into any model trained on it. |
| [isaac-sim/IsaacLab](https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab) | NVIDIA's GPU-parallel robot learning framework on Isaac Sim: articulated robot assets from URDF/USD, locomotion and manipulation environments, RL training with domain randomization. | The simulation target for Phase 6b dynamic walking training. Needs an NVIDIA GPU; our URDF (generated from `cad/skeleton.json`) is the import path. MuJoCo stays the lightweight local check. |
| [SoundSpaces 2.0](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sound-spaces) (added P-066) | Meta's audio simulator on Habitat: binaural room impulse responses path-traced on arbitrary meshes, validated against measured rooms (Chen et al. 2022, arXiv 2206.08312). | Room acoustics for the binaural hearing channel of docs/42, after the direct-path model of the robot's own contacts. MuJoCo has no acoustics. |
| [google-deepmind/mujoco_warp](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_warp) (added P-066) | MuJoCo on NVIDIA Warp, with a batch renderer that ray-traces MuJoCo primitives on the GPU; it replaced the deprecated Madrona-MJX. | Rendering the head cameras of docs/42 for vision training if CPU rendering is too slow; also the route for hub task `tendon-physics-on-gpu`. |

## How these fit the roadmap

| Phase | Simulator / scenario source |
| --- | --- |
| Now (kinematics, CoM, reach) | Blender rig from `cad/skeleton.json`; URDF export for a quick MuJoCo load |
| 5 (stand, balance) | MuJoCo: the tendon-level model of `13` with the balance controller of `14` and the single-support controller of `15` (stands, 30 N pushes, sustained 15 N, arm hold, squat, weight shift, 10 s one-foot hold: the Phase 5 exit test passes in simulation); microban's foot and stance conventions |
| 6a (static walk) | MuJoCo on the tendon model: the first static step of `16` (D-029) completes, every leg line inside its rating; microban walk engine as a reference |
| 6b (dynamic walk) | PPO on the fast stand-in (`tools/export_standin.py`) in the sibling project, judged on the tendon model (`17`, D-031); domain randomization over servo stiffness (a slack run halves it), damping, clutch torque, friction and masses, plus actuation delay; reward terms and randomisation after roboparty_train (BSD-3-Clause, Roboparty roboto_origin) |
| 3-4 (manipulation) | LeRobot for recording, datasets and policies over our ESP32 nodes; DreamDojo-style world models only if teleoperation data accumulates |
| 5 onward (generic policy core) | the sibling repository's `humanoid_tendon32` embodiment (P-011) wraps `tools/sim_tendon.py`: 32 controls over the firmware cascade, tensions, slack, slip, locks, IMU and foot loads as named sensor channels |
